# Format library

A **format** is a proven way of framing a promise (C-21). Each entry records **why** people click it, who it fits and what execution it demands, along with **positive and negative evidence** (C-22) and our own results. `/yt-research` and `/yt-upgrade` add to this file.

**About the evidence:** "+ (talk)" marks examples and numbers as Jake Trinder reported them. They haven't been verified. Before betting on a format, log real examples with `yt.py add <url>` and measure its base rate with `yt.py pattern "<regex>"`.

**Levers** are the working vocabulary for decoding why a format gets clicked. Add to the list as you go.

| Lever | The viewer thinks… |
|---|---|
| Speed | "faster than I thought possible" |
| Unfair advantage | "this feels like cheating" |
| Missing-knowledge threat | "if I don't know this, I'm doing it wrong" |
| Shift / urgency | "the game changed and I might be behind" |
| Proof of volume | "they did it at a scale I never will, so I'll learn from that" |
| Rawness / transparency | "they'll show everything, nothing hidden" |
| Stakes / challenge | "can they actually pull it off?" |
| Blunt honesty | "finally, someone will tell me the truth" |
| Leverage | "a big result from a tiny input" |
| Recency | "this is the current playbook" |

## Title formats

### TF-01 · How to [result] so fast it feels like cheating
- **Promise:** a result they already want, faster than they expected.
- **Levers:** speed and unfair advantage.
- **Fits:** viewers with a strong, existing desire for a known outcome who want it done or gone fast.
- **Execution it demands:** a genuinely fast, practical method up front. Slow theory breaks the promise.
- **+ Evidence:** "How to lose visceral fat so fast it feels like cheating" by Dr. Sean got 795K views and "broke their email system" (talk). Alex Hormozi and other top creators have used the frame (talk).
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "feels like cheating"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-02 · If you don't understand [X], you don't understand [Y]
- **Promise:** a hidden mechanism (X) unlocks the outcome they want (Y).
- **Levers:** missing-knowledge threat and curiosity.
- **Fits:** solution-aware markets, where people want Y but have never thought about X. Combined with C-15, this is how you present an unfamiliar mechanism through the outcome.
- **Execution it demands:** actually connect X to Y step by step. The viewer must leave understanding Y better.
- **+ Evidence:** "If you don't understand insulin, you don't understand fat loss" by Dr. Tessa got 230K+ views. It helped her reach 1M long-form views in 90 days and 14 clients, more than $50K net, in month one (talk).
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "don.?t understand .* you don.?t understand"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-03 · The new rules of [domain]
- **Promise:** the old playbook stopped working, and here's the new one.
- **Levers:** shift/urgency and recency.
- **Fits:** domains where the audience feels things are changing (marketing channels, platforms, careers).
- **+ Evidence:** Neil Patel did "new rules of SEO" (talk). "New rules of YouTube" was also done (talk). "New rules of LinkedIn" got about 70K views in the LinkedIn niche (talk). "New rules of social media" for Gary Vee got about 300K, about 10× his previous average (talk).
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "new rules of"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-04 · Brutally honest advice about [domain] in [N] minutes
- **Promise:** the unvarnished truth, quickly.
- **Levers:** blunt honesty and speed.
- **+ Evidence:** the social media version for Gary Vee, and a web design version at about 230K (talk).
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "brutally honest"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-05 · If I started [X] in [year], I'd do this
- **Promise:** the expert's restart plan with today's knowledge.
- **Levers:** recency and leverage.
- **+ Evidence:** the Gary Vee social media version was still a 10×+ outlier on his channel (talk).
- **− Evidence:** it did worse than his other social media formats (talk). Run `yt.py pattern "if i (started|were starting)"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-06 · The new era of [X] has just begun
- **Promise:** a new era has started, and here's how to be early.
- **Levers:** shift/urgency and recency.
- **Origin:** Trinder invented it by keeping the psychology of the shift formats (TF-03, TF-07) and giving them a new frame (C-26).
- **+ Evidence:** Tommy Clark's LinkedIn version got about 20–30K, an outlier in a small niche with many customers (talk). A startup or startup-funding version got hundreds of thousands (talk). Gary Vee's version about the move from social media to *interest media* got 400K+, against a previous average of about 10K (talk).
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "new era of"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-07 · [X] is about to change forever (and nobody even realizes)
- **Promise:** a big change is coming that others don't see yet.
- **Levers:** shift/urgency and insider knowledge.
- **+ Evidence:** "Social media is about to change forever and nobody even realizes", by a creator transcribed as "Callaway" (talk). Its *thumbnail* format was reused for Gary Vee's "new era" video (talk).
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "change forever"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-08 · I [did a huge amount of X] and learned this
- **Promise:** lessons from a scale of experience the viewer will never reach.
- **Levers:** proof of volume.
- **Execution it demands:** **20–40 points** (EX-01). Copies that used a 3–5 point listicle failed (talk).
- **+ Evidence:** "I made 300 YouTube videos and learned this" got about 500K (talk). There were also "I sent 10 million cold emails and learned this" and "I made 500,000 cold calls and learned this" (talk).
- **− Evidence:** 3–5 point versions failed (talk). Run `yt.py pattern "and (here.?s what i )?learned"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-09 · Speedrunning [goal] from zero (show everything)
- **Promise:** watch the entire process from zero to the first result, with nothing hidden.
- **Levers:** rawness/transparency and stakes.
- **Execution it demands:** a raw, complete video (EX-02, TH-03). A polished summary breaks the promise (C-33).
- **+ Evidence:** Glen Coco's first video got about 300K views and $100K+ in 30 days, and their first 12 videos made about $1M in 6 months (talk). The exact title is garbled in the transcript: something like "speedrunning … from zero, the first sale, show everything".
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "speedrun"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-10 · Watch me [achieve X] in [N] hours
- **Promise:** a real-time challenge with a hard deadline.
- **Levers:** stakes/challenge and rawness.
- **Execution it demands:** EX-03. The product can appear where it removes the challenge's bottleneck (CTA-01).
- **+ Evidence:** Instantly's "Watch me sell a service in 10 hours" made hundreds of thousands of dollars, and commenters praised it as "the best ad ever" (talk).
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "watch me"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-11 · The best [X] strategy for [year]
- **Promise:** the current, complete strategy.
- **Levers:** recency.
- **Execution it demands:** a complete strategy plus an asset that helps implement it (CTA-02).
- **+ Evidence:** Sam's "The best Facebook ads strategy for 2026", paired with a free ad library as the opt-in (talk).
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "best .* strategy (for|in) 20\d\d"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TF-12 · How I [big result] with only [tiny input]
- **Promise:** a big result from a small input.
- **Levers:** leverage.
- **+ Evidence:** Ryan Deiss ("Ryan Dice" in the transcript) with "How I run a $10M+ per year business with only one hour per week", paired with a scorecard Google Sheet as the opt-in. Trinder says he bought a ~$16K program from that funnel himself (talk).
- **− Evidence:** none logged yet. Run `yt.py pattern "with only"`.
- **Our results:** none yet.

## Thumbnail formats

### TH-01 · White text on a red highlight block
- **Why it works:** high contrast reads instantly, especially over a dark background (C-23). Trinder credits The Diary of a CEO with popularizing it.
- **Risk [INFERENCE]:** so many channels copy it that it can blend into the feed. Check it in the feed test.
- **+ Evidence:** used on the visceral fat video (talk).
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TH-02 · Visible transformation + authority cue + an audience-matched subject
- **Why it works:** the outcome is obvious at a glance, the cue (a doctor in scrubs) proves authority without words, and the subject tells the viewer "this is for me".
- **+ Evidence:** the visceral fat thumbnail showed a body transformation, a male body for a mostly male audience and Dr. Sean in scrubs (talk).
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TH-03 · Raw and unpolished
- **Why it works:** it signals "real, unedited, everything shown", and it must be paired with a raw video (C-33).
- **+ Evidence:** Glen Coco's speedrun video (talk).
- **Our results:** none yet.

### TH-04 · "About to change forever" shift thumbnail
- **Why it works:** it signals urgency and an insider view. It can be paired with a different shift *title* (C-26).
- **+ Evidence:** the thumbnail from TF-07, combined with the TF-06 title for Gary Vee's 400K+ video (talk).
- **Our results:** none yet.

## Execution formats (C-34)

### EX-01 · 20–40 point lessons
Required by TF-08. The volume of points is part of the promise.

### EX-02 · Raw full process, showing everything
Required by TF-09. Use real time with minimal polish. Editing only removes dead time.

### EX-03 · Time-boxed challenge with real stakes
The challenge creates the constraints, and the product (if there is one) removes one of them (CTA-01).

### EX-04 · "The only [X] video you'll ever need"
A full course. The Plan in the hook lists everything covered, so viewers commit to the length (C-32).

### EX-05 · Podcast with a spoiler intro
The intro previews the best moments so viewers know what the hour gets them (C-32).

## CTA formats (C-40 to C-44)

### CTA-01 · Product as the constraint remover
The video creates a real problem and the product solves it within the story. Instantly is the example: pre-warmed domains removed the two-week warm-up inside the 10-hour challenge.

### CTA-02 · Lesson-extension asset
After a strategy lesson, give the tool that helps the viewer act on it, such as an ad library or a KPI scorecard sheet, in exchange for their email. Email does the selling.
